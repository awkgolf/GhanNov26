# GhanNov26
Second trip down under starting from Darwin

## Geology framework

Use the Darwin–Adelaide journey as a transect across the long geological history of central Australia. The landscapes seen from the train are clues, not always direct exposures of the rocks beneath them: broad plains may conceal basin sediments, while ranges expose older and more deformed rocks.

### A route-based storyline

- **Darwin and the Top End:** Begin with ancient basement and the Paleoproterozoic rocks and mineral systems of the Pine Creek region. Consider how later weathering and tropical conditions shape the present landscape.
- **Across the Northern Territory:** Follow the transition through broad sediment-covered plains and older basement provinces. Use the Tennant Creek region as a prompt to discuss ancient crust, deformation, and mineralisation.
- **Alice Springs and the MacDonnell Ranges:** Read the ranges and surrounding basins through the Amadeus Basin’s sedimentary history and the later Alice Springs Orogeny, which uplifted and deformed rocks across central Australia.
- **Into South Australia:** Track the changing balance between basin cover and older cratonic rocks. The Gawler Craton, Stuart Shelf, and Adelaide Fold Belt provide regional context for the country approaching Port Augusta and Adelaide.

### Questions to carry through the journey

At each landscape transition, ask:

1. What landform is visible, and what might be hidden beneath the surface?
2. Is the story mainly about deposition, burial, deformation, uplift, erosion, or weathering?
3. What does the landscape suggest about the age and strength of the underlying rocks?
4. How have climate and water altered the geological record?

Treat these stops and provinces as a broad interpretive framework rather than a claim that every unit is exposed beside the railway. Check route maps and local geological sources when tying a view to a specific formation or outcrop.
